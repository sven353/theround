The Paradigm Shift

Most peer groups process issues. The Round closes them.

Senior operators rarely lack advice. What they lack is a room that compresses a live decision into a clear call, in front of people who will ask about it next month.

Traditional peer group

70 minutes of open issue processing

  • FormatUnstructured block that expands to fill the time available.
  • QuestionsMeandering anecdotes and advice disguised as questions.
  • DebateCircular alignment where everyone agrees the issue is complex.
  • OutcomeSympathy, a page of notes and the same decision still open.

The Round

40 minutes of disciplined decision work

  • FormatTimed phases with a facilitator who holds the clock.
  • QuestionsA strict question-only phase before any view is shared.
  • DebateForced trade-offs argued in front of the protagonist.
  • OutcomeDecisive prescriptions and a committed call with a deadline.

The Protocol

The 5-Phase Speed Advisory Protocol

One protagonist, one decision, one clock. Every phase has a single job and a rule the facilitator enforces.

5′ 8′ 12′ 10′ 5′

  1. Part 1

    Context & Framing

    The protagonist sets the board up to help.

    The Baseline
    2–3 operational facts that define the situation.
    The Friction
    Option A versus Option B, stated plainly.
    The Cost of Inaction
    What decays over the next 90 days if nothing is decided.

  2. Part 2

    Clarifying & Stress-Testing

    Strict rule: questions only.

    Allowed
    Open questions that expose assumptions, numbers and blind spots.
    Blocked
    Zero opinions. Zero disguised advice. The facilitator stops any "have you considered".

  3. Part 3

    Open Deliberation & Pressure Test

    The board debates. The protagonist listens.

    The Board
    Argues the trade-offs directly, in front of the protagonist, and disagrees openly.
    The Protagonist
    Stays silent and takes notes. No defending, no correcting.

  4. Part 4

    The Hard Call Round

    30–45 seconds per member. One prescription each.

    "If I were in your chair, I would decide [X], execute by [Y], and accept consequence [Z]."

  5. Part 5

    Commitment & Ownership

    The protagonist closes the loop in front of the board.

    Final decision
    Declared out loud, in one sentence.
    Named deadline
    A date, never a quarter.
    Accountability partner
    One peer who follows up before the next Round.

Time split is indicative. The facilitator adjusts it to the weight of the decision on the table.

Dynamic Facilitation Toolkit

One board. Four formats across the year.

The Speed Advisory Board is the core format. Across the year the facilitator rotates in other formats so the group builds range as well as speed.

Speed Advisory Board

The five-phase protocol above. Two or three protagonists per session, each leaving with a committed call.

Best for
Time-bound binary decisions
Block
40 minutes per protagonist
Output
Decision, deadline, partner

Systemic Deep-Dive

Longer issue processing for problems with many moving parts: a leadership team that keeps re-litigating decisions, a culture that fractured after a raise. The board maps the system before anyone prescribes.

Best for
Recurring, tangled friction
Block
75 to 90 minutes, one protagonist
Output
System map and first intervention

Governance Stress-Test

The board plays investors, directors or a hostile acquirer. The protagonist rehearses a board meeting, a down round narrative or a CEO succession conversation under real pressure.

Best for
Board, investor and succession moments
Block
45 minutes with role assignments
Output
Hardened narrative and red-flag list

Operational Sprint

Every member brings one execution bottleneck. Rapid rounds of 10 minutes each, focused on the next concrete move and the metric that proves it worked.

Best for
Execution speed across the group
Block
10 minutes per member
Output
One next move per member

Annual progression

  1. Q1

    Calibrate

    Speed Advisory Boards establish the protocol, the tempo and the level of candor.

  2. Q2

    Go deeper

    Systemic Deep-Dives take on the friction that a single decision cannot solve.

  3. Q3

    Pressure

    Governance Stress-Tests ahead of budget season, fundraising and board cycles.

  4. Q4

    Execute

    Operational Sprints and a year-end review of every committed call on the tracker.

Participant Caliber

The board is only as sharp as the people around it.

Seats are curated for fit. We look for operators who will bring real decisions and give real answers.

Ideal fit

Operators with skin in the game

  • Founders and C-level operators running companies of 5 to 2,000 people.
  • An international perspective, with experience across markets or cultures.
  • A bold posture toward decisions and a bias for committing.
  • Radical candor, both given and received.
  • Willingness to expose genuine operational friction, including the uncomfortable parts.

Out of scope

Anyone here to watch

  • Passive networking seekers looking for introductions or deal flow.
  • Spectators who want to hear other people's problems without bringing their own.
  • Those seeking theoretical coaching detached from P&L reality.

If you are looking for one-to-one executive coaching, Kuma Partners offers that separately at kuma.partners.
